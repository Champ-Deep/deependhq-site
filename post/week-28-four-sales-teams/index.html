Four sales teams, one operating rhythm.

What happens when you stop managing channels and start running them like products.

On Monday morning the sales floor had four leaders and one Monday meeting. By Friday it had four leaders, four Monday meetings, and one shared dashboard. The thing nobody tells you about restructuring is that the structure is the easy part.

For most of the last three months I had been treating our sales org as four channels into the same machine: Phoenix, Assassins, Synergies, Prodigies. The names are silly on purpose. The work, less so.

Group operators don't manage twelve things. They build one system that runs twelve.

What changed this week

The old setup had one VP of sales across all four teams and four team leads reporting up. Reasonable, on paper. In practice: every conversation about one team ended with a tangent into another, every tangent surfaced a third team's blocker, and the VP became a routing layer for problems that did not need routing.

So I did the only honest thing. I dissolved the routing layer.

>_ shipped this week

  • Four leads, four P&Ls, four standalone Monday meetings.
  • A shared dashboard that doesn't lie about overlap.
  • One operating doc that says when to escalate and when to just run it.

The harder part

The harder part, and this is the part I am still in the middle of, is that four leaders need four different things from me. One wants air cover. One wants me out of the room. One wants me in the room, but quietly. One wants the playbook for the third time.

Twelve months ago I would have called this a management problem. Now I think it is a design problem. The system has to make space for four different relationships to me. The dashboard, the doc, the meeting cadence: those are the affordances. If I am the one keeping it together, it is not a system. It is a bottleneck wearing a system costume.

If you're the one keeping it together, it isn't a system. It's a bottleneck in a costume.

What I'm watching next week

Whether the four Monday meetings stay four Monday meetings. The forcing function for collapse is always the same: a deal that touches two channels and someone has to decide. If the decision rule is in the doc, we keep four. If the decision rule is "ask me," we collapse by week 30.
