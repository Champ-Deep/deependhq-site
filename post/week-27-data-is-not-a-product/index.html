Data is not a product. Growth is.

How a category gets created in a single Monday meeting, after six months of saying it wrong.

For six months we said "we sell data." This week we stopped. The repositioning landed in one conversation, and the hard part was not the language. The hard part was the six months of saying it wrong first.

Lake B2B has always done more than sell records. We build the data, generate the demand, and run the delivery. Three layers. But we kept describing ourselves by the cheapest, most commoditized one. Buyers heard "data vendor," compared us to every other list seller, and anchored on price.

If buyers compare you on price, you described yourself with the wrong noun.

The reframe

We are not a data company that also helps with campaigns. We are a B2B growth stack: data, demand, delivery, in one place. The data is the engine, not the product. The product is pipeline. Once you say it that way, the comparison set changes. You are no longer next to list sellers. You are next to the whole motion a buyer would otherwise stitch together from five vendors.

>_ the three layers

  • Data: the records, cleaned, enriched, intent-scored.
  • Demand: the campaigns that turn records into conversations.
  • Delivery: the infrastructure that lands it and the reporting that proves it.

Why it took six months

Category language is not a tagline you choose. It is a claim you have to be able to back. We could not credibly say "growth stack" until the demand and delivery layers were real enough to point at. The slogan was downstream of the build. The six months were not wasted on indecision. They were spent earning the right to the sentence.

You don't pick a category. You build until one is true, then you name it.

What changes now

Every surface gets rewritten around "enabling growth," with the stack as the proof underneath. The sales team stops leading with record counts and starts leading with the motion. And the next six months are about making the new sentence so obviously true that nobody asks for the old one.
