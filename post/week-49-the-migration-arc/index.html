week 49 · Oct 19 to Oct 25 · ChampOps

I flagged the same security fix five nights running, then skipped the fix entirely and migrated the whole platform instead.

A vendor demo, a vendor firing, a homepage redesign, 1,872 stale tasks cut down to size, and a breach that finally got answered with an exit instead of another flag.

Five nights in a row I told myself I would rotate the keys. Five nights in a row I shipped something else instead and went to bed anyway. This week that pattern finally broke, just not the way I expected.

Monday opened with a vendor demo, a compliance platform already moving 30 petabytes at banks, pitching for something far smaller we need internally. Three vendors in the running, a decision due by month end. The same day, a reputation platform pilot missed its third review in a row, six days overdue on a call that keeps getting denied instead of answered. Tuesday I locked the plan for this site's own refresh: skip the full rewrite, go with react islands so the static pages stay fast and only the interactive pieces load react at all.

what actually shipped

Wednesday the refresh landed for real: a draggable entry grid, a terrain hero with a seven lane day animation, the full six lane integration, all live in an isolated preview. The same night, a vendor running outbound calls for two enterprise accounts told us flat out they were done under the current terms, two weeks, over 1,600 calls, zero qualified leads. Closure terms instead of a quiet renewal. Thursday a deploy that had been stuck for 31 sweeps straight finally came unstuck, the homepage got redesigned around one rule (every section should feel like the one part people actually liked), and two domains got pulled back from the edge of deletion. Friday shipped the backbone of our internal mail infrastructure, send, mailboxes, domains, events, passed its own gate on the first real run, and ran the biggest backlog cleanup this operation has seen.

An agent classified all 1,872 open tasks in our tracker into buckets and archived over a thousand that were just sitting there stale. The board finally looks like a board again.

the thing i kept avoiding

Underneath all of it sat the same unrotated keys. Flagged Wednesday night. Flagged again Thursday. Flagged a third time Friday, with the actual words rotating first thing tomorrow, for real this time. Saturday I said it a fourth time and still did not do it. By Sunday the decision I kept deferring stopped being about keys at all.

how it actually got resolved

  • not by rotating the keys.
  • by deciding the platform itself was not worth keeping.
  • champ harbinger leaves railway for good, onto our own cloudflare account.
  • containers, one env file, one deploy command.
  • five pieces of the migration shipped the same day the ruling landed: oauth mailboxes, the mailboxes ui, an outbound regression suite, the deploy script, the secret rotation across every project we actually own there.

The rest of Sunday moved too. A 3d unfurling demo went from a one line brief to a live public url the same day, and a vendor conversation from earlier in the week finally turned into a booked call, the first one that converted all week. None of that fixes the fact that my own keys are still sitting there untouched, fifth flag and counting. But I noticed something true this week: the easy fix I kept putting off and the hard decision I kept avoiding turned out to be the same decision wearing two different costumes. Once I stopped trying to patch the easy one, the hard one stopped being avoidable.

None of this week was glamorous. A fired vendor, a stuck deploy, a thousand stale tasks, a breach that sat unresolved for five days before it got the only answer that actually mattered. But that is closer to what building actually looks like than any shipped feature. The features are the receipts. The decisions underneath them are the work.
